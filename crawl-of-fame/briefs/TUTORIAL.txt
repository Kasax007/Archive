TASK __ID__: Orientation, the tutorial inside the dungeon. The owner of the mod played the interim build and wrote: "we are lacking a tutorial on first join", and then: "I meant the tutorial in the dungeon", in the way the genre does it: new crawlers are received on the first floor by someone who works there and are walked through how this place functions. Today the Gaffer stands in a room somewhere on floors 1 and 2 and answers questions if a player happens to find him, and hints appear as toasts: a new player sees neither. You build an orientation nobody can miss and nobody is forced through twice. Everything about it is our own: our Gaffer, our studio, our jokes; no scene, line or character from any book, game, film or show (tools/dev/check_names.py must pass). Reports to read: A8-npc (the Gaffer, dialogue, hints, welcome kit, the TUTORIAL room), A1-world and B8-stagedoor (how a floor's entry is built, Stations and Spots), A2-run (members, floors, the clock), B2-profile and A6-magic (stats, skills, spells), B3/B4 (gear, crates, booth, stash), B6-map, A5-show (Emcee, Hype), UI1 (the kit; docs/UI.md is binding) and, if it is merged, the report of the items task (Power, the item card, equipping) and of the voice task (how a line gets a voice).

THE IDEA (decided): the first floor of a run begins backstage, in the Rehearsal Wing: a short, linear suite of small sets built in front of the first green room, where the Gaffer takes new talent through a rehearsal before the cameras roll. One beat per set, each beat one thing done by the player's own hands, each done in under a minute. The Gaffer walks ahead (or waits at the next mark), talks in his dry way (subtitled; voiced once the voice task has run: register every line with it), and the quest tracker shows the beat ("Rehearsal 3/9: Hit the dummy with a charged blow"). The floor's clock does not run in the wing and no monster of the floor enters it.

DELIVER
1. The wing as part of floor 1's generation (world module: a hook, listed under shared changes): eight to ten small sets in a row with their own look (a rehearsal space: marks taped on the floor, a mirror wall, costume rails, a tea urn, cue lights; build it from the kit of blocks that exists, add props where a beat needs one), ending at the curtain into the floor proper. Lit, readable, no dead ends, wide enough for a cast of eight. Every cast gets the wing; who has graduated walks straight through (see 4).
2. The beats (write them as data: id, the Gaffer's lines, the task, how the server knows it is done, the next beat; so that pack makers can change the order). Suggested, improve freely, but each of these things must be taught by doing:
   - Welcome: who he is, what the show is, what winning and dying mean here (ten floors, the stairs, the clock; death ends the run and the Remains keep what you carried).
   - Your sheet: open the crawler screen, spend the first stat point (a free point is given for it), look at a skill.
   - Arms: a rack with one weapon of each basic kind to try on dummies that show damage numbers: a plain blow, a charged blow, a block with a shield, a shot or a throw; the crawler keeps the one they choose. The Gaffer says in one sentence what each kind is good at.
   - Wearing things: a locker with a piece that is better than what the crawler wears: read the card, see the upgrade mark, put it on (the items task's verdict line and Power, if merged).
   - Magic: one starter spell on the bar, cast at a target; mana and cooldown explained by seeing them.
   - Prizes: a rehearsal crate, the Unboxing Booth in the wing, open it; what crates are and where they come from.
   - Your stash and the green room: put something in the stash, eat or drink, a bandage; a vendor's hatch: buy one thing with the coins handed out. What a green room does (safe, heals, the sidekick returns, if that module exists).
   - The map: open it, set a waypoint to the stairs symbol; the minimap; what the room symbols mean.
   - The audience: Hype explained with a small crowd flat that cheers when the crawler does something showy; the Emcee cuts in once over the PA (his first line to this crawler).
   - Dress rehearsal: one weak monster let out of a prop cage (it cannot kill: the Gaffer stops it at low health and the crawler is healed), then the curtain: "Places."
3. Detection and comfort: every beat completes by what the player does, never by a button "next"; a beat that is not done within a while gets a second, more direct line and a marker in the world (a light on the thing to use, the key's name in a KeyHint). Dying is impossible in the wing. The beats work with a controller-less keyboard and mouse at any key binding (take key names from the bindings).
4. Who gets it: a flag per player in the saved profile data ("graduated"), set at the curtain or by skipping. Graduates find the wing open and the Gaffer nodding them through with a short line (several variants); they may still use the dummies. Skipping: the Gaffer's first topic "I know the ropes", the command /crawl orientation skip, and config orientation (on, off, always). /crawl orientation replay resets the flag. In a mixed cast nobody waits: the curtain is never locked, the tracker belongs to each crawler alone, and walking out through the curtain mid-rehearsal counts as skipping after one confirming line. The kit a crawler gets (the chosen weapon, the locker piece, the crate, coins, the starter spell) replaces today's welcome kit: once per crawler and run, graduates get it from the Gaffer in one talk.
5. After the wing: the things a first floor cannot teach arrive when they first matter, by the Gaffer's voice over the crawler's earpiece (one line, subtitled, a small portrait in the kit's lower third) instead of today's toasts: the first rule of floor 2, the first shrine, sponsor offer, role and makeover on floor 3, a boss door, the first Remains, the clock at ten minutes. Once per player ever, as today (keep /crawl hints and the config).
6. The Gaffer himself: a walk between marks that looks like a walk (no sliding, no teleporting in view), turning to the player, a gesture per beat (pointing, a thumb over the shoulder, arms crossed while waiting) with the animation system that exists (if the creature task has merged, use its clips; otherwise keep it simple and say so).
7. Tests: game tests for every beat's detection, the kit once per run, graduate walks through, skip paths, mixed cast, relog in the middle of a beat (resumes at that beat), a restart, the clock not running in the wing, no monster entering, death impossible. Crawltest script tools/crawltest/scripts/orientation.txt: a new player goes through every beat with pictures of each (Gaffer, tracker, marker), a graduate walks through, at GUI 2 and 3. Look at every picture: would a player who has never seen the mod know what to do next? Copy the sheets to /home/user/shots/__ID__/.
8. Docs: docs/DESIGN.md (the wing, the beats), docs/CONFIG.md, docs/DATAPACKS.md (beats as data), README.md (first steps: point to the wing).

Texts: about ninety lines of the Gaffer in all (beats, second hints, graduate variants, earpiece lines), in his voice: dry, kind, a stagehand with forty seasons behind him; short sentences that can be spoken aloud in under eight seconds each; never a wall of text; no fourth-wall jokes about being a tutorial.
