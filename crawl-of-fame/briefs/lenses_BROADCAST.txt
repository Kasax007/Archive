breaks: a programme cut from live data, on a screen drawn in the world
The server records moments of a run, cuts a running order for everybody in a green room and the clients draw it on a wall of blocks. Try to break it: the record (unbounded growth over a long run: fight for an hour with tools/dev/load.sh or a scratch test and measure its size and the saved data; moments of a crawler who left the cast, died, was removed by an admin; moments that name a monster, a place or an item removed by a data pack; a run saved before this task existed); the running order (an empty night, one crawler, eight crawlers, crawlers of two casts in one room, a crawler who enters in the middle of a segment, relogs, or leaves while it plays: do all see the same picture at the same second, and does the last one out stop the work); what the season hides (does any caption, picture, silhouette, colour or sound give away a hidden place, and does a crawler of another cast's deeper season see it); names (a cast mate's name or position shown to somebody outside that cast; a player name with odd characters, sixteen wide letters, a skin that fails to load); the screen (a wall of 1x1, 2x2, 8x5, a wall with a missing block, a block broken by an admin while it plays, a screen seen through a doorway from the corridor, from behind, from 60 blocks, in third person; the picture when the client's GUI scale, window size or fullscreen changes; resource reload with F3+T; the cost: frames per second with no screen, one and four screens in view, measured the way the report says, and the memory after entering and leaving green rooms twenty times: a render target that is never freed is a finding); the sound (heard outside the room, after leaving, after death; two screens in one room; the screen over the Emcee, over a person, over the earpiece: tools/crawltest/listen.py); the built jar (tools/dev/prod.sh client: the screen draws there too); a dedicated server without a client's classes; state in statics; packets unbounded or trusted. Write scratch game tests under /tmp and report what fails.

television: would a player stop and watch, and stay for the next segment
The owner asked for the show "automatically generated and being aired on a big TV in the green rooms" and wrote "this would be fun". Watch it as a player in survival (the task's scripts and your own: a green room after a quiet floor, after a busy one, and a third visit in the same run; 960x540 and 854x480 at GUI 2; from two and from eight blocks; default brightness) and take one picture per second through two whole running orders. Judge every segment kind: does it look like television made by one channel (a logo, lower thirds, wipes, an ident: one house style drawn with the kit's tokens and type) or like a slide with text; is it readable from eight blocks and crisp from two (text sizes, contrast, nothing cut by the frame, nothing that flickers or swims as the camera moves); is it about the viewer (their face, their numbers, their kills) and true (check three captions against what really happened in the run); does anything move that should (bars ease in, numbers count, cards slide, a model turns) and is the motion calm; are the captions written like a show (dry, quick, our own: nothing that names or echoes a real programme, channel, book, film or game) and free of repeats over three visits; does a quiet night still give a reason to look; is the anchor's every line worth hearing (read each against its direction in the voice script) and does a muted screen still tell everything; does the screen belong in the room (its frame, its glow, the seats facing it) in green rooms of every size; does the stand-by picture look intended. For part 2 also: the full-screen view against docs/UI.md (one panel at a time, buttons with hover and pressed states, text that fits at GUI 2 and 3 and on 480x270 GUI pixels) and each sponsor's spot as an advertisement somebody would remember. Compare with the pictures in /home/user/shots/__ID__/. Report what is dull, unreadable, untrue or looks unfinished, with the picture.
