TASK __ID__: items in three dimensions. The owner of the mod played the interim build and wrote: "Items and armours need more 3D models and in general everything needs more polished textures." "More animations on items too." "More item models." "The shield does not look right in the hand because it is not a 3D model." Today every item of the mod is a flat sprite, held like a sheet of paper; a shield is a sprite on the arm; all six armour sets are the vanilla armour shape with another colour; nothing on an item moves. You give the gear of the mod real shapes, textures worth looking at, and life. Reports to read: B3-items (bases, uniques, sets, the item scripts in tools/content/item), B4-crates (crates, booth, trunk), A3-mobs (how tools/content/mob builds box models and paints textures by script: the same thinking applies), AUDIO-sound (sounds on use). Look first: /home/user/shots/B3/, /home/user/shots/B4/ and the creative tab of the mod in a crawltest picture of your own, held items in first and third person.

HOW (decided)
- Every piece of gear keeps a flat icon for slots (GUI, and a fixed frame) and gets a model for the world (in the hand, first and third person, on the ground, on the head): one item definition that selects by display context, as the game does for its own spyglass and trident (grep the game's assets for "display_context": do not guess the format of 26.3).
- Models are written by script (tools/content/item/models.py -> models/item/*.json): built from boxes with rotation, small enough for the game's limits, each face mapped to a texture the same script family paints. A weapon is not a sprite extruded to one pixel of depth: a blade has a ridge and an edge, a guard stands out, a grip is wrapped, a pommel is a ball; a mace has flanges; a halberd has a spike, an axe blade and a hook; a bow has limbs that bend.
- Nothing copied from any pack, mod or game; our own shapes.

DELIVER
1. Weapons: models for every melee base (shortsword, cleaver, mace, dagger, hatchet, prop sabre, greatsword, maul, halberd, boom pole), the shortbow and crossbow (with their pulling and loaded states as the game does it for its own), the throwing knife (in the hand and as the flying entity, spinning), wand and staff (a head that glows in the school of the bound spell if that is known on the item; otherwise a neutral glow). Held right: grip in the fist, two-handed bases held with both hands in third person (pose the off arm where the game allows it; say what you could do), sensible first-person position per type, a size that matches its reach.
2. Shields (the owner named this): buckler and riot shield as models strapped to the arm, with a raised blocking pose in first and third person (the game's own shield shows how the item definition switches on "using"), a face texture with a boss, rim and rivets, the unique shields with their own face. In first person the shield must not hide the middle of the screen while blocking.
3. Uniques: every unique item (Uniques.ALL) is its own model, recognisable at a glance and different in silhouette from its base (not only in colour): it is the reward of a boss.
4. Armour with shape: the six sets get their own geometry on top of the body: a hard hat with a lamp, shoulder pads, a tool belt, knee pads, a cape, a visor, a helmet crest, a tall collar, whatever makes the set what its name says; read ArmorSet and the texts and design each set as a costume from the studio's wardrobe. Use the armour rendering hook of the Fabric API version this project builds against (look in the sources jar for ArmorRenderer or its successor in 26.3; do not guess), with models built in code from a description written by script, attached to the right body parts so that they follow the player's animation, sneaking, swimming and the sidekick-free second layer of skins; correct on players, armour stands and the crawler model of the crawler screen. Boots and leggings too. No z-fighting with the skin's outer layer; the vanilla armour layer under it is switched off for these sets.
5. Trinkets on the body: a ring is too small to see, but the charm hangs at the belt and the badge sits on the chest when slotted (the trinket slots are known to the client: B4).
6. Life (client only, cheap):
   - held: a slow idle sway is the game's; add what the item does itself: the wand's head pulses, the staff's orb turns, a unique's rune glows in a slow breath, the riot shield's lamp blinks, a charged heavy blow makes the blade shimmer while it is ready. Driven by the game's item model conditions and tints where they suffice, by a small special renderer where they do not.
   - used: a swing leaves a short arc in the rarity or element colour of the weapon (a few quads, fading in 6 ticks; off with reduced motion), blocking flashes the rim on a blocked hit, a throw has follow-through.
   - in the world: dropped gear lies tilted on the floor instead of spinning as a sprite, with a soft beam of light in its rarity colour from rare upwards (height by rarity, visible through a crowd of monsters but not through walls) and a small sparkle; coins lie as little stacks. A legendary or unique drop gets a chime (exists in the audio module or add one).
   - in slots: the icon of a legendary and of a unique has a slow sheen passing over it (one sprite animation by .mcmeta or a drawn overlay in the kit's decorator: ask the items-UX report which decorator exists).
7. Textures to match, for everything this task touches and for every other item of the mod while you are in the scripts (supplies, bombs, crates, keys, treats, coins, quest items: about 125 icons today): one light from the upper left, three or four steps of shading per material, a dark outline that is not black, highlights on metal, grain on wood, no single-pixel noise; an icon must read at slot size at GUI scale 2 from arm's length. Make a sheet of all icons before and after, on dark and light slot backgrounds, and repaint what is muddy.
8. Pictures, the proof of this task: a crawltest script tools/crawltest/scripts/models_tour.txt: every weapon and shield held in first person and third person (front and three-quarter view), blocking, a bow drawn; every armour set worn, full and mixed, standing, walking, sneaking, on an armour stand; every unique; drops of each rarity on the floor in a dark room; the crawler screen's model wearing a set. Sheets that put old next to new. Look at every picture at full size; a model that looks like a thick sprite, floats beside the hand, clips through the arm or the body, or is told from another only by colour is not done. Copy the sheets to /home/user/shots/__ID__/.
9. Cost: the item models stay within the game's baked model path (no per-frame mesh building); the special renderers allocate nothing per frame; a hall with forty monsters and twenty drops must not drop frames because of this task (say how you measured). Docs: docs/ITEMS-ART.md (one page: how to add a model, a texture, an armour shape; the quality bar with two pictures).
