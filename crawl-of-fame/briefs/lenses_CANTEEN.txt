breaks: plates, recipes and seats, used by people who want two of everything
The cook hands one plate per green room visit, the fridge and the urn give once per floor, recipes are kept across runs, crawlers sit down. Try to cheat and to break it: a second plate (a relog in the green room, a server restart, leaving and re-entering, dying and coming back, a cast mate handing theirs over, the offer screen closed and reopened hoping for other dishes, two crawlers at the hatch in the same tick); a bonus that never ends or stacks with itself (eat, relog, eat a snack, drink the coffee, a dish of another place carried along: can it be carried at all); a plate eaten in a fight, during a boss introduction, while falling, while dead; the eating clip interrupted by a hit, a screen, a slot switch, a death (is the dish spent, and is that fair); ingredients duplicated or lost by the specials (a full bag, a relog while the cook cooks, a recipe that asks for what the crawler no longer holds); the recipe card of one player shown to another; seats (sitting into a wall, sitting on a seat whose block is gone, two crawlers on one seat, a relog or a restart while sitting, a crawler who is hit, teleported, escorted by the run's guard or carried to the next floor while sitting, a sidekick stuck in a chair, a monster that sits); the cook hurt, pushed out from behind the hatch, killed by an area effect, missing after a restart; the kitchenette in every size of green room and in the Rehearsal Wing's backstage (nothing blocks the booth, the vendors, the stairs or a Spot; the screen wall is free); packets trusted without checks (a plate asked for from another room or another floor, a dish that was not offered, a seat out of reach); state in statics; chunks loaded by timers. Then the numbers: with tools/dev/balance.sh at depths 2, 5 and 9, do the bands of docs/BALANCE.md hold with the strongest dish and without any, and is a plate worth choosing at all? Write scratch game tests under /tmp and report what fails.

feel: is the cook somebody, and is a meal something a player does
The owner asked for kitchens "where NPCs can give me food" and cares most about animation, textures and clean interfaces. Visit the canteen as a player in survival (the task's scripts and your own; depths 1, 2 and 5 in two places; 960x540 and 854x480 at GUI 2 and a GUI of 480x270; default brightness) and judge: the cook from the door, from the hatch and in a strip of the idle, frame by frame (a body and a face of their own, busy hands, a look up when the crawler comes near: or a statue behind a counter); every spoken line read aloud against its direction in the voice script (a temper, things only a cook would say, no line that names or echoes a book, film, game or show, no line that repeats what the screen says); the offer (three cards: can a player choose in five seconds, does each dish say what it does and for how long, is the dish of the place recognisably of that place, do the pictures look like food drawn with care); eating in first and third person as a strip (a plate in the hand, bites, an empty plate: nothing slides, snaps or floats; other players see it); the kitchenette next to the green room's other furniture (one light direction, shading in steps, steam and small motion, nothing that looks like a vanilla block; the specials board readable and changing); seats (the crawler sits on the seat and not in it, the camera does not look into a wall, standing up is obvious); the recipe card and the screens against docs/UI.md (one grid, even spacing, hover and pressed states, text that fits at GUI 2 and 3, one panel at a time); the sounds (the bell, a pan, cutlery: heard and not annoying on the tenth visit; a listening run with tools/crawltest/listen.py: the cook is understood and the Emcee does not talk over the cook).
