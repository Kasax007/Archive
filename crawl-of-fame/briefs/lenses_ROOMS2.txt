exploits: games with prizes, played by people who want the gold without the game
Bonus rounds pay crates, coins and Fans, are played once per crawler or cast, and are judged by the server. Try to cheat and to break them: a game played twice (a relog, a server restart, a death and a new run through the same door, the floor's set struck and rebuilt, two crawlers pressing the button in the same tick, a cast mate who joins the cast or leaves it in the middle, a crawler who enters after the start); the prize taken twice or lost (a full bag, a relog on the plinth's rise, leaving through the curtain as the fanfare plays); a game left in a state that never ends (everybody dead, everybody gone, the only player disconnected, the button pressed during the prize); the curtain as a wall (a crawler or a sidekick locked in, a monster of the floor walking in, a called monster walking out or still alive after the end); scores forged by packets (a hit on a lamp that is not lit, a step on a tile from across the room, a cash-out after the bust, a bid of coins the crawler does not have, an answer after the clock, every index out of range, a sender who is not in the room, not a crawler, dead); the reflex games at 150 ms and 400 ms of delay (is the forgiveness fair and can it be abused by a client that lies about its delay); gadgets, spells, sidekicks and the room's own set pieces used inside a game (does a gadget win the Spotlight Stand, does a summoned helper hit lamps, does a blink skip the course); a game inside a place whose rule is active (the manor's dark, the tide, the cold: does the rule hurt, drown or freeze a player who must stand still for the game, and does the game's light count as the rule's light); state in statics; chunks loaded by a game's timers. Then the numbers: with the prize tables and tools/dev/balance.sh, check the report's claim about what the bonus rounds of a floor are worth against a district of fights at depths 1, 5 and 9, and that no fight's number moved. Write scratch game tests under /tmp and report what fails.

play: is each game a game, and is its rule clear from the room
The owner asked for more to do than fighting and loves variety. Play every game of this task as a player in survival (the task's scripts and your own; three places: a studio department, Hollowmere Manor and one more; 960x540 and 854x480 at GUI 2 and a GUI of 480x270; default brightness) and write down for each: what a newcomer understands within five seconds of stepping through the curtain without reading anything (the rule card, the tape marks, the lamps: do they say what to do and what counts); what the hands do and whether that differs from every other game (two games that are both "stand on the right tile" are one game); whether every action answers at once (a sound, a light, the scoreboard) and whether the end is an event (the fanfare of the tier, the plinth, the Emcee, the crowd) or a chat line; whether bronze is reachable by a first-time player and gold is hard for a practised one; whether a loss stings without punishing; whether it works alone and whether a cast has something to do together or only waits for turns; whether the show's set looks carried into the place's room in each of the three places or erases the place; whether the scoreboard is readable from the far wall and the rule card at the small GUI; whether the Emcee's line can be understood under the game's sounds (a listening run: tools/crawltest/listen.py) and never talks over a person; whether the map symbol, the floor panel's chip and every HUD part obey the layer rule (tools/crawltest/scripts/hud_layers.txt); whether anything flickers, floats, clips into the place's props or looks like a vanilla block. Look at the strips frame by frame. Compare with the pictures in /home/user/shots/__ID__/. Report what a player would not understand, would find dull or would call unfair, with the picture.
