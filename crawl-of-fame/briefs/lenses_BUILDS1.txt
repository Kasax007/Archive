balance: seventeen techniques against the numbers the game stands on
Every weapon type has a technique and a trait now. Find what is too strong, too weak or broken: for every type compute damage per second with and without its technique, reach, block and control from the code and from tools/dev/balance.sh at depths 1, 5 and 9 with the same item level and Power, and list them side by side (a type that wins everywhere, or one nobody would pick, is a finding); loops (a technique that resets its own cooldown, a cancel that doubles a hit, a slot switch or a relog that skips the cooldown or the recovery, a technique used during another, while blocking, casting, riding, falling, in a screen, during a boss card or in a green room); techniques on bosses (does a stagger, a trip, a pull or a push skip an act, interrupt an introduction, lock a boss for a whole fight or move it out of its arena: every boss of three places), on other crawlers, sidekicks and people; thrown and ranged techniques through walls and gates; the hatchet that never comes back, knives that vanish or double; the vault and the dash as ways out of a cell, into a closed arena or a vault; two-handed weapons with an off-hand piece (the lead decided: both hands are taken): every way to get the off hand's block or stats anyway; the rehearsal dummy's numbers against a real monster's and against a hand count; Power and the item card (does the technique's line tell the truth); packets trusted without checks (a technique from a weapon that is not held, faster than its cooldown, from a dead or absent crawler). Write scratch game tests under /tmp and report what fails, with the numbers.

feel: does each weapon type play differently in the hands
The owner asked for builds to try and says weapons are hard to understand. Take every type into a fight in survival (a script of your own on the rehearsal dummies and against three monsters; strips with the crawltest's strip command at 1280x720 GUI 2 in first and third person, and two clients seeing each other: tools/crawltest/mp.sh): is the technique's wind-up, hit and recovery readable frame by frame (name the clip and the frame where it snaps, slides, floats beside the hand or passes through the body); does the hit land where the picture says; is its sound its own (a listening run, CT_AUDIO=1); is the key shown where the player looks and the cooldown readable at 480x270; does a newcomer learn the key in the Rehearsal Wing; does the item card say in one line what the move does, and is that true; does the dummy's panel answer "which of my two weapons is better" in ten seconds. Compare opposites (the dagger and the maul, the buckler and the greatsword, the wand and the staff) and say whether a player would feel the difference by timing alone. Report each technique that plays like another with a different number, and each that a player would never press twice.
