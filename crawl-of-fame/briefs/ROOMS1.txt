The owner of the mod played the interim build and wrote: "functional blocks or stuff to loot in the dungeons, more interactive rooms or stuff to do instead of run from monster to monster." Today a room is monsters with a chest here and there; the dressing is scenery nobody touches. After this task a crawler who walks into any room of any place sees things to do besides the fight, and the fight itself is fought with the room. Since the brief was first written the dungeon has changed: a floor is a PLACE drawn per run (docs/PLACES.md, report THEMES1: eleven places today, ten studio departments and Hollowmere Manor, which is another world; more worlds follow) at a DEPTH that only says how strong things are. Everything you build is placed by place and scaled by depth, never by a floor number. Reports to read: THEMES1, W1-manor (the first place that is not a studio set: its props, its droppable chandeliers, its sideboards), B5-dressing, B7-dressing23 and the floor reports F4 to F10 (what stands in the rooms, how looks and compositions place it, Spots and Stations), B3/B4 (loot tables, chests, lockers, prop crates), ITEMS (Power, item level, the item card), B9-rules (traps, secrets, objectives, fight modifiers), A3/A4 (monster AI, abilities, what monsters react to), A5-show (Hype, Emcee occasions), B6-map (room marks), TUTORIAL (the Rehearsal Wing's beats, the Gaffer's hints), UI1 and docs/UI.md (the kit, key hints, "One panel at a time", client.ui.Layers and the script hud_layers.txt), MODELS2 and docs/ITEMS-ART.md (the clip system for hands and held items), docs/VOICE.md. THE BAR: the first room a newcomer enters after this task teaches itself. Within five seconds they see what can be searched and what can be used against the monsters, without a text. Each kind looks like the thing it is and looks different once it is used. Fewer kinds made excellent beat many that are boxes with a new texture: if you must choose, cut the count, not the quality, and say so.

DELIVER
1. Searching (one mechanic for all of it): a prop that can be searched shows it (a soft glint every few seconds, the kit's key hint when looked at from close: "[use] Search"), holding use fills a ring for half a second to two seconds (by prop; Agility and a matching skill shorten it) while the crawler's hands rummage (a clip of the clip system, seen by other players too), the prop changes visibly when done (drawer out, lid off, rail empty) and stays so for the run. Loot is rolled per crawler: every member of a cast may search every prop once and sees their own state, so nobody races a friend. Searching makes a little noise: while a fight is on in the room it draws the nearest monster. The ring and the hint are HUD parts of the kit and obey the layer rule (register them with client.ui.Layers; extend tools/crawltest/scripts/hud_layers.txt), and they fit a GUI of 480x270.
2. At least twelve searchable or breakable kinds, as blocks with art by script (to the standard of docs/ITEMS-ART.md and the common rules: one light, shading in steps, a shape that reads at a glance) and their own loot tables (data pack). They are placed by the looks of EVERY place in fitting numbers (a hook in world, listed under shared changes) and docs/PLACES.md gets a section that tells every later place how to get them. The studio's departments share kinds in their own skins: costume rail (a wearable or coins), prop crate (exists: now breakable by weapon, with debris), tool chest (materials, a trap part), filing cabinet (in the Sunken Archive: reels and notes; elsewhere coins and a fan letter), first-aid box on the wall (bandage, antidote; one per district), craft-service trolley (food), lost-and-found bin (junk, rarely a trinket), cash register (coins; rings a bell: draws monsters), safe (needs a key that some monster on the floor carries, marked by a small key icon over it, or a lockpick try with Trap Handling: good loot), camera case (a consumable), mail sack (fan letters: Fans), make-up desk (a short charm buff), vending machine (exists: keep). A place that is another world gets kinds of its own instead of studio props: Hollowmere Manor needs at least six that belong in a manor (a writing desk, a linen chest, a silver cabinet, a coat stand, a coal scuttle, a tea trolley, a wall safe behind a portrait: your choice, in its walnut and brass), and its sideboards of tapers stay what they are. Average value per room is set so that a crawler who loots thoroughly earns about a third more than one who only fights, and the item level follows the depth (docs/BALANCE.md gets a paragraph; do not inflate gear: mostly supplies, coins, materials, cosmetics).
3. The room as a weapon: things in combat rooms that hurt monsters when a crawler uses them well, placed by the compositions (two to four per larger combat room, fewer in small ones, none in green rooms, none in the Rehearsal Wing except the one it teaches with). One mechanic per class, with a skin and a name per place where the studio's would look wrong:
   - droppers: hanging lights and sandbags on ropes (hit the rope anchor or shoot the rope and it drops on whatever stands on the taped mark below: damage by depth, stuns). Hollowmere Manor's chandeliers already drop when their cleat is cut (ManorRule, ManorStages): bring them under this mechanic and its look without changing what the manor's bosses need;
   - exploders: pyro crates (explode when hit or shot, chain to neighbours, set oil alight; marked clearly; hurt crawlers too); in the manor a cask of lamp oil;
   - spills: oil drums that spill a slick when knocked over (slows, burns when lit), water mains that flood a patch (conducts shock spells and the fuse box's sparks);
   - a fuse box that overloads the room's floor lamps for three seconds when pulled (shocks everything standing on metal grating; a cooldown of a minute);
   - a stage trapdoor with a lever (opens under what stands on it; monsters fall into the pit below and climb out hurt after a while, or die on spikes at greater depths);
   - a safety curtain on a chain that splits a room in two for twenty seconds (in the manor a portcullis or a heavy drape);
   - a spotlight on a stand that can be turned and switched on: blinds monsters in its cone, and whoever fights in the light gains Hype faster;
   - a wind machine (pushes monsters and projectiles down a lane), a fog machine (monsters lose track, crawlers too);
   - the traps that exist (rules.trap) become usable on purpose: a lever or a shot sets them off while monsters stand in them.
   Every piece shows what it will do before it does it (the taped mark, the paint, the lever, a line from the anchor to the mark when looked at) and has a wind-up a player can read. Monsters do not use these, but the clever kinds avoid a lit mark when they can (read the AI and add what is cheap). Bosses are not trivialised: say per piece what it does to a boss (reduced damage, no stun during an introduction or an act change, no pit). Kills made with the room count for the crawler, pay more Hype ("Set kill"), train a new skill "Stagecraft" (add it to profile: listed under shared changes) and have their own Emcee lines (add them to the voice script with a direction: docs/VOICE.md; the lead records) and three achievements.

The Rehearsal Wing teaches it: one prop to search and one piece to use against a dummy, as a beat of its own or inside an existing beat (TUTORIAL: tracker, skip and replay keep working), and the Gaffer's hint the first time a crawler who skipped the wing looks at either.
