THE FRAME (decided)
- A room kind "Bonus Round" that the layout hands out in every place at every depth: two on a small floor, three on a medium one, four on a large one; never on the critical path (a crawler in a hurry loses nothing); never next to a boss gate; the games of one floor all differ.
- The show's set in the place's room: walls, floor, ceiling and light are the place's own (its dressing: a hook in world, listed under shared changes), the game's furniture is the show's and is the same everywhere (one set of art by script, to the standard of the common rules): a tinsel curtain in the doorway, an ON AIR lamp over it, a podium with one big button, a scoreboard on the wall, a prize plinth, cables and tape marks on the floor. It must look carried in: a game-show set standing in a manor's panelled room is the joke.
- Entering: through the tinsel curtain. The Emcee names the game and its rule in one line (voice script, with a direction, three variants per game); a rule card on the wall says it again in three pictograms and one sentence; the podium's button starts the game. Solo games are played once per crawler, cast games once per cast (everyone in the room plays, the prize scales with the number of players). A crawler who comes late watches. Monsters never enter a bonus round except where the game calls them, and what a game calls is gone when it ends.
- Ending: three prize tiers (bronze, silver, gold) from one table per game and depth (crates, coins, Fans, now and then a cosmetic; leave a hook for a perk token of the later builds task and name it), handed over on the prize plinth with a fanfare of its tier. Losing never kills by itself and never locks anybody in: a lost game opens the curtain. Leaving through the curtain during a game ends it as lost for that crawler; a relog during a game counts as lost; a server restart during a game counts as not played. docs/BALANCE.md gets the expected value per depth, set so that playing every bonus round of a floor is worth about as much as one more district of fights. No number of the fights changes.
- The scoreboard shows the score live, readable from across the room (drawn in the world; read how the map and the item models draw in the world before you choose how), and keeps the cast's best for the run.
- The show notices: Emcee lines for the start (per game), a gold, a bust and a near miss (voice script), the crowd's sounds that exist, Hype for a medal, "games won" in the recap and on the Hall of Fame board, four achievements. The map shows a bonus round with its own symbol once it was seen from the door, ticked when played.
- Saved per crawler and cast (played, medal, best score), safe across relog and restart. No way to leave the cell or to trap a crawler. The server judges everything; the client only shows. A game of reflexes measures on the server and forgives a normal connection (say how, and test it with a delayed player).

THE FIRST FIVE GAMES (each with its own room layout inside the place's walls, its rule, its sounds, its lights, its three tiers; playable alone and by a cast of up to eight; replace one with a better idea if you have it, but keep the spread)
1. Hit Your Marks (memory and feet): a floor of lit tiles; a sequence lights up with a tone per tile and grows by one each round; walk it without a wrong step. A cast takes the rounds in turn and shares three lives.
2. Shooting Gallery (aim): pop-up flats on rails for sixty seconds; friendly flats cost points. The booth hands every player bean bags to throw for the length of the game (so that no build is locked out and none has an edge), and takes them back.
3. Hot Seat (nerve): a pot that grows with every press of the button and a rising chance of a loud, harmless bust that loses it; cash out at any time. The room shows the odds honestly (a row of lamps: one more goes red with every press). Every player has a seat and a pot of their own; the others watch.
4. Lights, Camera (reflexes): a wall of lamps; hit the lit one with whatever is in the hand before it goes out; the pace rises; a combo counter on the board.
5. Spotlight Stand (a fight with a rule): hold the lit circle for forty-five seconds while monsters of the place pour in; the circle moves; the score is the time spent in the light.
