echoes: every text and every spoken line read for what was borrowed
Read ALL of it, not a sample: print the texts module by module in a compact form (key and text, a few hundred per page) and read every one; then the spoken script the same way. You are looking for anything a reader would recognise as coming from somewhere else: a quoted or bent line of a film, a series, a song, a play, a book, a game, an advertisement, a real programme or a real presenter ("shaken, not stirred" and "final answer?" were in there until today); a name that is a character, a place, a product, a band, a title or a real person, or one letter away from one; a monster, an item, a spell or an achievement whose idea is one particular work's (not a stock figure of adventure stories: a mummy, a mimic, a tin soldier are free; a particular film's scene or a particular game's enemy is not); a joke that only works because the reader knows the original; a sponsor that is a real brand in a thin disguise. Plain idioms and terms of the trade are fine and are not findings ("break a leg", "the show must go on", "cliffhanger", "director's cut"): the test is whether it belongs to somebody. Where you are not sure, say so and say what it reminded you of. For this lens the limit of eight findings does not hold: one finding per text, each with its key, the text, what it echoes and a replacement of our own in the same voice (the Emcee's dry showman, the Gaffer's tired stagehand, a vendor's patter: read ten neighbours of the line first). Then add the phrases you found to your report as a list for docs/NAMES_NOT_ALLOWED.txt. Check the designs too: docs/DESIGN.md, docs/FLOORS.md, docs/WORLDS.md, README.md.

language: every text read as a copy editor reads, and as a new player reads
Read ALL of the lang files and the spoken script (compact pages, every entry). Three things. (1) Mistakes: typos, doubled or missing words, wrong grammar, a sentence that ends in the middle, a placeholder shown raw (%s, %1$s where the code passes nothing, a key instead of a text), wrong plurals with counts ("1 crates"), texts that contradict what the game does today (a number, a key, a rule that has changed: check ten of the most specific claims against the code or the data), spelling that is not British (the texts say colour, armour, favourite, cancelled). (2) Words for the same thing: a player reads "floor", "depth", "level", "set", "stage", "place", "department", "district", "zone", "room", "arena"; "crawler", "contestant", "cast", "cast mate", "party"; "Fans", "Hype", "airtime", "the clock"; "crate", "prize", "trunk", "stash", "bag"; "Power", "item level". For each family say what each word means in the game, list where one word is used for two things or two words for one thing in what a PLAYER sees (not in code), and propose the one usage; the worst confusions first (a tutorial line, a HUD label and a card that name the same thing differently are a finding each). (3) Clarity for somebody who has never played: every hint, every tutorial line of the Gaffer, every card of a rule (docs/PLACES.md: each place's rule) and every item, stat, skill and spell description: does it say what to DO and what will happen, in one reading, without a word that is explained nowhere? List the twenty worst with a rewrite in the same voice. For this lens the limit of eight findings does not hold either: group by kind, list every key. A text you change must still match its recording where it has one (tools/content/voice/build.py compares them): say which rewrites would need a new recording.
